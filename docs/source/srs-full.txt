Purpose of This Document 
This document expands the original ABG-SSCOS vision statement into a vendor-ready 
Enterprise User Requirement Specification (URS) and Software Requirement Specification (SRS). 
It is intended to be issued as part of a Request for Proposal (RFP) so that prospective system 
integrators and technology vendors can size, design, cost, and commit contractually to the 
delivery of the Bashundhara Smart Sports City Operating System. 
Scope of This Document 
This document covers: business and technical context, the target system architecture, module-
by-module functional requirements, user stories, formal use cases, business process flows, 
screen/UI requirements, API and integration requirements, data architecture, key performance 
indicators, acceptance criteria, service level agreements, and cybersecurity requirements. It 
also defines the vendor evaluation approach to be used during procurement. 
How Vendors Should Use This Document 
● Respond to every functional requirement (FR) with a compliance status: Comply / Comply with 
Configuration / Comply with Customization / Not Compliant / Roadmap. 
● Reference requirement IDs (e.g., FR-TCK-004, UC-SEC-02) directly in the technical and commercial 
proposal. 
● Provide a mapping of proposed product modules against each layer of the reference architecture in 
Section 3. 
● Complete the acceptance criteria and SLA sections as binding commitments in the contract schedule.

1. Vision & Business Context 
1.1 Vision Statement 
ABG-SSCOS shall be a fully integrated, Artificial-Intelligence-powered Smart Sports City 
Operating System capable of operating, monitoring, automating, securing, analysing, and 
optimizing every aspect of Bashundhara Sports City from a single unified platform, delivering 
“One System, One Dashboard, One Source of Truth” 
The platform shall simultaneously function as an ERP, Command Center, Security Platform, 
Stadium Management Platform, Facility Management Platform, Customer Experience Platform, 
Digital Twin Platform, AI Decision Engine, Revenue Management Platform, and Enterprise Data 
Platform. 
1.2 Business Objectives 
Objective Description 
Unified Operations 
Eliminate departmental silos by operating stadium, retail, 
hospitality, security, and facility functions from one platform. 
Revenue Growth 
Increase ticketing, membership, retail, and sponsorship revenue 
through pricing, upsell, and analytics. 
Visitor Experience 
Deliver frictionless, contactless, personalized experiences across 
booking, entry, and on-site services. 
Safety & Security 
Provide proactive, AI-assisted threat detection and incident 
response across the entire city's footprint. 
Operational Efficiency 
Reduce manual effort and downtime through predictive 
maintenance and automated workflows. 
Data-Driven Governance 
Give executives real-time, trustworthy KPIs for evidence-based 
decision-making.

1.4 Guiding Design Principles 
● Modular, API-first architecture that allows phased rollout and future extensibility. 
● Single source of truth: no duplicate master data across modules. 
● Security and privacy by design, aligned to ISO 27001 / ISO 27701 / SOC 2 / OWASP. 
● AI-assisted, human-supervised automation — no fully autonomous action on life-safety 
events without human confirmation, except where explicitly approved SOPs allow it. 
● Cloud-ready, on-premises-capable hybrid deployment to satisfy data residency 
requirements. 
● Vendor-agnostic integration layer to avoid lock-in on IoT, CCTV, and access-control 
hardware. 

2. Scope

● Design, development/configuration, integration, testing, deployment, and warranty 
support of ABG-SSCOS across all layers described in Section 3.

devices. 
● Content creation for marketing, sponsorship media, or event production.

3.1 Non-Functional Architecture Requirements 
Attribute Requirement 
Availability 
99.9% uptime for Layer 1/2/3 transactional systems; 99.95% for Layer 
4/6 life-safety and security functions (see SLA Section 32). 
Scalability 
Support horizontal scaling to a minimum of 100,000 registered visitor 
profiles and 20,000 concurrent app/portal sessions during peak events. 
Latency 
Access-control decision (face/QR/RFID scan to turnstile release) shall 
complete in ≤ 1.5 seconds at the 95th percentile. 
Interoperability 
All modules shall expose RESTful JSON APIs and support webhook-based 
event publishing (see Section 27). 
Deployment Model 
Hybrid: Cloud-hosted application tier with on-premises edge nodes for 
CCTV/IoT processing and local failover. 
Data Residency 
Personally identifiable information (PII) of Bangladeshi citizens shall be 
stored in-country unless otherwise approved by ABG legal/compliance. 
Disaster Recovery 
RPO ≤ 15 minutes, RTO ≤ 4 hours for core transactional systems; RPO ≤ 1 
hour, RTO ≤ 1 hour for SOC/life-safety systems.

Must

Must 
FR-TCK-006

Student, Corporate) including renewal and auto-billing. 
Must

The system shall generate a unique QR/RFID/NFC credential per 
ticket or membership, valid for a configurable time window. 
Must 
FR-TCK-008 
The system shall allow refunds, cancellations, and ticket transfers 
subject to configurable business rules. 
Should 
FR-TCK-009 
The system shall support dynamic and promotional pricing rules 
(early-bird, off-peak, bundle) configurable by admin. 
Should 
FR-TCK-010 
The system shall reconcile ticketing revenue with Finance ERP 
General Ledger on a daily automated batch. 
Must 
FR-TCK-011 
(NEW) 
In addition to the QR/RFID/NFC credential in FR-TCK-007, the system 
shall support an optional wearable wristband/tag as a physical 
credential and cashless payment instrument, linked to the same 
visitor wallet used for ticketing and in-facility purchases (see FR-FNB-
005). The wristband shall operate via the RFID/NFC modalities 
already defined for turnstile authentication in FR-ACC-005 — no 
separate access-control mechanism is introduced. 
Should 
FR-TCK-011 is new in v2.0. It extends the existing credential/payment model rather than 
duplicating FR-ACC-005 (multi-modal turnstile authentication), which already supports 
RFID/NFC as a scan method.

24. Use Cases 
The following formal use cases illustrate end-to-end system behavior for the highest-risk and 
highest-value scenarios. Vendors shall confirm their proposed solution can execute each flow, 
including all listed alternate/exception paths, during the proof-of-concept stage. 
UC-TCK-01 — Purchase a Sports Ticket and Gain Venue Access 
Field Detail 
Actors Visitor (Primary); Payment Gateway, Access Control System (Supporting) 
Trigger Visitors want to play a sport (e.g., Padel) at Bashundhara Sports City. 
Preconditions Visitor has a registered account or completes registration during booking. 
Main Flow: 
● 1. Visitor opens the App/Web Portal and selects the desired sport and slot. 
● 2. System displays available slots and pricing. 
● 3. Visitor selects a slot and proceeds to payment. 
● 4. System processes payment through the integrated payment gateway. 
● 5. System issues a digital ticket with QR/RFID credential and sends confirmation via 
SMS/Email. 
● 6. On the day of visit, visitor presents the QR code or face at the turnstile. 
● 7. System verifies identity/credential, checks blacklist status, and confirms booking 
validity. 
● 8. System grants access and logs entry with timestamp. 
Alternate / Exception Flows: 
● 3a. Payment fails → system retains slot hold for 10 minutes and prompts retry. 
● 7a. Blacklist match found → system denies access and alerts SOC immediately (see UC-
SEC-01). 
● 7b. Booking expired or slot mismatch → system denies entry and directs visitors to help 
desk / AI concierge. 
Postconditions: 
Visitor is inside the venue; entry is logged; revenue is recorded against the correct sport and 
cost center. 
UC-SEC-01 — Detect and Respond to an Unauthorized Access Attempt 
Field Detail 
Actors 
AI Access Control Engine (Primary); SOC Operator, Security Field Team 
(Supporting) 

Field Detail 
Trigger 
An individual attempts to enter a restricted zone without valid credentials, 
or a blacklisted identity is recognized at any access point. 
Preconditions 
Face recognition and blacklist databases are active and synced; access 
point is online. 
Main Flow: 
● 1. Individual presents at an access point (turnstile, restricted door). 
● 2. System scans identity via face/QR/RFID/NFC. 
● 3. System checks identity against permission rules and blacklist database. 
● 4. System determines the individual is not authorized for the zone. 
● 5. System denies physical access and raises a real-time alert to the SOC dashboard with 
camera feed reference. 
● 6. SOC operator reviews the alert and dispatches the nearest security field team. 
● 7. Field team resolves the situation and logs the outcome in the incident module. 
Alternate / Exception Flows: 
● 4a. Individual is a recognized VIP without pre-registered zone access → system prompts 
SOC for manual override with reason capture. 
● 6a. No field team available within SLA → system auto-escalates to duty manager. 
Postconditions: 
Access denial and resolution are logged; incident record created if escalated; blacklist database 
updated if new threat identified. 
UC-INC-01 — Automated Fire Detection and Emergency Response 
Field Detail 
Actors 
AI Incident Detection Engine (Primary); Fire Team, Command Center, 
Visitors (Supporting) 
Trigger 
Smoke/fire signature detected via CCTV video analytics or integrated fire-
alarm sensor. 
Preconditions 
Fire detection sensors and/or video analytics are operational and 
integrated. 
Main Flow: 
● 1. System detects a fire/smoke signature above the confidence threshold. 
● 2. System classifies the incident as “Fire” and determines the affected zone. 
● 3. System automatically triggers the Fire SOP workflow. 

● 4. System alerts the Fire Team, Security, and Command Center simultaneously via app 
push and SMS. 
● 5. System displays the incident on the Executive/Security dashboard with live camera 
feed and evacuation zone map. 
● 6. Field teams respond; system tracks response time from detection to first responder 
arrival. 
● 7. Once resolved, the responsible officer files a closure report with root cause and 
actions taken. 
● 8. System logs resolution time and closes the incident. 
Alternate / Exception Flows: 
● 1a. False positive suspected → SOC operator can downgrade/cancel the alert with 
mandatory justification, logged for audit. 
● 4a. Primary responder team does not acknowledge within SLA → system auto-escalates 
to secondary responder and management. 
Postconditions: 
Incident fully logged with timestamps, responders, and resolution; feeds into post-incident 
analytics (FR-INC-007). 
UC-FAC-01 — Scheduled Facility Maintenance with Escalation 
Field Detail 
Actors 
Facility Management System (Primary); Maintenance Staff, Facility 
Manager (Supporting) 
Trigger 
Scheduled maintenance/cleaning task becomes due (e.g., swimming pool 
cleaning). 
Preconditions Maintenance schedule and SOP checklist configured for the asset. 
Main Flow: 
● 1. System auto-generates the task per the configured schedule. 
● 2. System assigns the task to the designated staff member and sends a notification. 
● 3. Staff member performs the task and submits completion evidence (checklist + photo) 
via mobile app. 
● 4. System verifies completion against the SOP checklist. 
● 5. System marks the task complete and updates the asset maintenance history. 
Alternate / Exception Flows: 
● 3a. Task not completed within the SLA window → system sends a reminder, then 
escalates to the Facility Manager if still overdue. 

● 4a. Submitted evidence fails verification (e.g., incomplete checklist) → system reopens 
the task and notifies the assigned staff. 
Postconditions: 
Asset maintenance history updated; overdue-task escalation log available for audit and vendor 
SLA reporting. 
UC-BI-01 — Executive Natural-Language Query via AI Copilot 
Field Detail 
Actors Executive User (Primary); AI Copilot / BI Engine (Supporting) 
Trigger 
Executive wants an operational answer without navigating multiple 
reports. 
Preconditions 
User is authenticated with an executive role profile; underlying data 
pipelines are current. 
Main Flow: 
● 1. Executive opens the AI Copilot chat panel on the Executive Dashboard. 
● 2. Executive types a natural-language question, e.g., “How much revenue did we earn 
today?” 
● 3. System interprets intent, checks the user's data-access permissions, and queries the 
curated data warehouse. 
● 4. System returns a direct answer with the supporting figure, time range, and a link to 
the underlying report. 
● 5. Executive may ask a follow-up question in the same context (e.g., “Break that down 
by revenue stream”). 
Alternate / Exception Flows: 
● 3a. Requested data is outside the user's authorization → system declines and logs the 
attempt. 
● 3b. Underlying data pipeline is delayed → system states the data's last-refreshed time 
rather than presenting stale data as current. 
Postconditions: 
Executive receives an accurate, permission-scoped answer; query is logged for AI Copilot quality 
monitoring. 
UC-VND-01 — Automated Vendor Revenue-Share Settlement 
Field Detail 
Actors Finance System (Primary); Vendor Partner, Finance Controller (Supporting) 
Trigger End of settlement period (e.g., daily or monthly, per contract) is reached. 

Field Detail 
Preconditions 
Vendor contract terms (commission %, settlement frequency) are 
configured in the system. 
Main Flow: 
● 1. System aggregates the vendor's recorded sales for the settlement period. 
● 2. System applies the contracted commission/revenue-share formula. 
● 3. System generates a settlement statement and posts it to the vendor's partner portal. 
● 4. System notifies the vendor and Finance Controller of the settlement outcome. 
● 5. Finance Controller reviews and approves the payment run. 
Alternate / Exception Flows: 
● 1a. Vendor disputes a sales figure → vendor raises a dispute in-portal; settlement is held 
pending Finance review. 
Postconditions: 
Settlement statement archived; payment instruction generated for Finance/banking 
integration. 
UC-FNB-01 — Order Food & Beverage via Location-Aware Ordering (NEW) 
Field Detail 
Actors 
Visitor (Primary); Indoor Positioning System, Outlet POS, Payment 
Gateway (Supporting) 
Trigger 
Visitor wants to order food or a drink without leaving their seat or current 
location inside the venue. 
Preconditions 
Visitor is inside the venue with an active session; at least one outlet near 
the visitor's zone is open and accepting orders. 
Main Flow: 
● 1. Visitor opens the Food & Beverage Ordering screen in the app. 
● 2. System determines the visitor's current zone via indoor positioning (FR-FNB-002) and 
lists nearby open outlets (FR-FNB-001). 
● 3. Visitor selects an outlet, browses the menu, and adds items to the cart. 
● 4. Visitor chooses a fulfillment mode: pickup point or location-delivery (FR-FNB-004). 
● 5. Visitor pays via the shared wallet/payment gateway (FR-FNB-005). 
● 6. System routes the order to the outlet's POS queue and displays a live status timeline 
(FR-FNB-003). 
● 7. Outlet prepares and marks the order ready; the visitor is notified. 

● 8. Order is fulfilled (collected or delivered); revenue reconciles automatically to the 
vendor's settlement record (FR-FNB-006). 
Alternate / Exception Flows: 
● 3a. An item goes out of stock while browsing → system reflects the outlet's real-time 
update (FR-FNB-007) and removes the item from the cart. 
● 7a. Outlet cannot fulfill within the SLA window → system offers reassignment to a 
comparable outlet or an automatic refund (FR-FNB-008). 
Postconditions: 
Order is logged end-to-end with timestamps and location; revenue is recorded once against the 
correct outlet, with no duplicate entry in the vendor settlement ledger.

27. API Requirements 
27.1 API Design Standards 
● All modules shall expose RESTful APIs using JSON payloads over HTTPS (TLS 1.2 or 
higher). 
● APIs shall be versioned (e.g., /api/v1/...) with a documented deprecation policy of at 
least 12 months' notice. 
● Authentication shall use OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect with short-lived access tokens and 
refresh tokens. 
● All APIs shall be documented using the OpenAPI 3.x specification and published to a 
developer portal. 
● Event-driven integrations shall be supported via webhooks and/or a message broker 
(e.g., Kafka/RabbitMQ) for high-volume telemetry (IoT, CCTV analytics). 
● All APIs shall implement rate limiting and quota management per consuming 
application. 
● Sensitive endpoints (access control, financial, PII) shall log every call with caller identity, 
timestamp, and payload hash for audit.

● Life-safety systems (fire alarm, emergency lighting) shall retain independent, hard-wired failure-

29.2 Master Data Management (MDM) 
● Visitor/Member identity shall be governed as a single master record across ticketing, 
access control, CRM, hospitality, and food/retail ordering — no duplicate identity silos. 
● Employee identity shall be mastered in HRM and referenced (not duplicated) by 
attendance, access control, and payroll. 
● Asset/Facility records shall be mastered in the Facility Management module and 
referenced by BMS, Finance (fixed assets), and Procurement. 
● Outlet/vendor records shall be mastered in the Vendor & Business Partner module 
(Section 15) and referenced, not duplicated, by the Food, Beverage & Retail Ordering 
module (Section 22A). 
● A data-quality rule set (mandatory fields, format validation, de-duplication matching) 
shall be enforced at the point of ingestion. 
29.3 Data Governance & Privacy 
● PII fields (NID/passport, biometric templates, health data) shall be encrypted at rest 
with field-level access controls, independent of general database encryption. 
● Biometric templates shall be stored as non-reversible mathematical representations, 
never as raw images, wherever the underlying face-recognition vendor supports it. 
● Data retention periods shall be explicitly configurable per domain (e.g., video analytics 
metadata 90 days per FR-SOC-007, access logs 12 months per FR-ACC-007) with 
automated purge. 
● Data lineage shall be traceable from any BI dashboard figure or AI Copilot answer back 
to its source system record. 
● Consent capture and withdrawal (e.g., for health/performance data under FR-SPT-004, 
or a minor's wristband pairing under FR-SOC-009) shall be recorded and enforced at the 
query layer. 
29.4 Reporting & Analytics Layer 
● A curated data warehouse (or equivalent data-mart layer) shall serve all dashboards to 
avoid placing analytical load on transactional databases. 
● Near-real-time refresh (≤ 5 minutes) shall be supported for Command Center 
dashboards; batch refresh (daily) is acceptable for deep historical/financial reporting. 
● The AI Copilot (FR-BI-002) shall query only the governed, permission-tagged data layer 
— never raw source tables directly.

33.2 Identity & Access Management 
● Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) shall be enforced for all administrative, financial, and 
SOC operator accounts. 
● Single Sign-On (SSO) via SAML 2.0 / OIDC shall be supported for enterprise identity 
provider integration (e.g., Azure AD). 
● Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) shall be enforced at both the UI and API layer, with 
least-privilege default profiles. 
● Privileged account activity (super-admin, database admin) shall be recorded and subject 
to periodic access review (minimum quarterly). 
● Session timeout, password complexity, and account lockout policies shall be 
configurable to ABG's security policy. 
33.3 Data Protection 
● Encryption at rest (AES-256 or equivalent) for all databases, file stores, and backups. 

● Encryption in transit (TLS 1.2+) for all internal and external network communication, 
including IoT/edge device links where technically feasible. 
● Field-level encryption and access segregation for NID/passport numbers, biometric 
templates, and health data, per Section 29.3. 
● Key management via a dedicated Hardware Security Module (HSM) or cloud KMS, with 
documented key-rotation procedures. 
33.4 Application & Infrastructure Security 
● Secure Software Development Lifecycle (SSDLC) including static (SAST) and dynamic 
(DAST) application security testing prior to each major release. 
● Dependency/software composition analysis (SCA) to detect vulnerable third-party 
libraries. 
● Network segmentation separating Tier 1 life-safety/security systems, Tier 2 
transactional systems, IoT/OT networks, and public-facing DMZ components. 
● Web Application Firewall (WAF) and DDoS mitigation for all public-facing endpoints. 
● Endpoint protection and hardening baselines for all operator workstations in the 
Command Center and SOC. 
33.5 Monitoring, Detection & Response 
● A Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platform shall aggregate logs 
across application, infrastructure, and network layers. 
● SOC monitoring (cyber, distinct from the physical Security Operations Center in Section 
6) shall provide 24/7 threat monitoring with defined escalation procedures. 
● AI-based threat analytics shall be used to detect anomalous access patterns, credential 
stuffing, and insider-threat indicators. 
● A documented Incident Response Plan (cyber) shall define roles, communication 
protocols, and regulator/customer notification timelines consistent with applicable law. 
● Immutable audit logs shall be retained for a minimum of 12 months (or per Section 29.3 
domain-specific retention) and protected from tampering. 
33.6 Third-Party & Supply Chain Security 
● All third-party hardware/software integrations (Section 28) shall undergo a security 
review prior to production connection. 
● Vendor personnel with access to production systems shall be subject to background 
checks and signed confidentiality/NDA agreements. 
● The vendor shall disclose its own subcontractors and hosting providers, including data-
center locations, for ABG's data-residency assessment. 
33.7 Governance & Assurance 

● Independent penetration testing shall be conducted at least annually and after any 
major release, per Section 31.1. 
● The vendor shall provide a right-to-audit clause allowing ABG or its appointed auditor to 
review security controls. 
● A responsible-disclosure / bug-bounty channel shall be established for reporting 
vulnerabilities. 
● Security awareness training shall be provided to ABG operational staff as part of the 
knowledge-transfer deliverables.
